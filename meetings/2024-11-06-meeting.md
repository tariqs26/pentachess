## Notes

#### Discussion of this week's action items

- For the board, we will use a hierarchical representation: the `Board` is an array of `Ring`s, which are arrays of `Side`s, which are arrays of `Cell`s.
  - We aren't sure about the usefulness of the `Side` construct, so we may revisit it.
- Each cell will need a variety of pieces of data, likely including the following:
  - Id (i.e. display number)
  - Coordinates (i.e. which ring it's in and which position it has within its ring)
  - Position (i.e. $x$ and $y$ coordinates on the screen)
  - Angle
  - Colour
  - Pointers to the next and previous edge-adjacent cells in the circle
  - Pointers to the inner and outer edge-adjacent cells in other circles
    - These don't always exist, so these must be nullable
  - A list of pointers to vertex-adjacent cells
    - In order to make certain operations easier, it will likely be easier if we split these into a few different arrays, e.g. cells in the same circle and cells in other circles.
    - Because the bishop's 'go in a straight line across 2 circles' rule is difficult to enforce, it may be helpful to have a separate construct just for this rule. I did it this way for the demo, at least.
  - The piece that is currently on the cell.
    - Together with this, we might store a list of cells that that piece could move to.
- Pieces
  - They should have a pointer to the cell they are currently on.
